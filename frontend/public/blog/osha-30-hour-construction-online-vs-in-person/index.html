OSHA 30-Hour Construction: Online vs In-Person

Compare online and in-person OSHA 30-hour Construction training for supervisors. Verify the provider, check card requirements, and choose the right fit for your crew.

For a construction supervisor who needs a 30-hour Outreach card, choose an online course from OSHA’s current Construction 30-hour provider list if scheduling flexibility matters most. Choose an in-person class with a currently authorized construction trainer if your crew needs to ask questions together. Neither option replaces instruction on the hazards of your actual jobsite. Compare your contractor safety program with the actual work before treating the card as the whole plan.

TL;DR
  • For OSHA 30 hour training online for construction supervisors, check OSHA’s current Construction 30-hour provider list before paying.
  • For an in-person class, inspect the trainer’s current construction authorization card and confirm the 30-hour course.
  • Ask the organization requiring the card whether it accepts your chosen course and temporary completion proof.
  • Neither a 30-hour card nor a 10-hour card replaces training on your crew’s actual jobsite hazards.

Best for one supervisor with a changing schedule: online. Best for a crew working through shared jobsite questions: in-person. This is a delivery choice, not a choice between a valid card and an invalid one. Check the provider or trainer and the requirement before you pay.

Check the card requirement before you book

OSHA’s Outreach FAQ says Outreach training is voluntary under federal OSHA, is not a certification or license, and does not satisfy the separate training requirements for employees’ tasks. Employers, contracts, states and local jurisdictions can still require a card. The organization imposing the requirement decides what it accepts.

Ask the hiring contractor or site owner three questions: Does it require Construction 30-hour rather than General Industry 30-hour or Construction 10-hour? Does it accept an online course from an OSHA-authorized provider? Will it accept a course-completion certificate while the student card is being issued? Get the answer before enrolling a supervisor whose start date depends on proof of training.

A card also does not tell you whether the written procedure matches the work. If you already have a program, compare the document with the current jobsite.

Online: verify the provider and the exact course

Open OSHA’s current online Outreach provider list. Find the Construction 30-hour section. Confirm that the company offering the course is named there, then confirm the enrollment page says Construction 30-hour Outreach. A search ad using the word “OSHA” is not proof that its provider is on that list.

Before checkout, ask how a student gets help with course questions, what happens if access fails, and how the provider handles completion proof and the student card. If you need proof for a job start, ask the organization requiring it whether a temporary certificate is acceptable; OSHA says acceptance of a certificate instead of the card is the jobsite’s decision.

Best for: an individual supervisor who cannot hold several full class days open. Limitation: course questions do not become a live discussion with your entire crew. The company still needs to walk its own tasks and controls with the people doing the work.

In-person: verify the trainer, not just the class name

Ask to see the trainer’s current Authorized Trainer card before booking. Check the name, construction authorization and expiration date. If the card is unclear, contact the authorizing training organization on it. OSHA’s trainer verification guidance says that organization can confirm status; OSHA does not operate a national trainer-verification database.

Confirm the class is Construction 30-hour Outreach, who teaches it, the location and schedule, and how student cards are issued. For a crew, put its real questions on the table before class: the tasks, the equipment and the controls supervisors need to check. Do not assume a general course will write your site procedure for you.

Best for: supervisors who need direct questions and a shared session. Limitation: a fixed schedule takes people off the work calendar. Choose the class for the instruction it gives, not because the classroom alone guarantees a different card.

Plan for the actual training time and card

The course requires 30 hours of instruction; it is not a one-day sign-off. OSHA limits in-person Outreach training contact time to 7½ hours per day, excluding breaks and lunch, so ask the trainer for a realistic schedule. For an online course, ask the provider about its course access and pacing rather than assuming the hours can be compressed.

OSHA says trainers must issue the student course-completion card directly to the student within 90 days after completion. If the project needs proof sooner, ask both the training provider and the party requiring the card about a temporary completion certificate. Keep a copy of the student’s proof in your records, but do not claim a certificate will be accepted everywhere.

Do not substitute OSHA 10 or the wrong industry track

OSHA describes the 10-hour course as basic hazard awareness; the 30-hour course covers more topics in greater depth and is more appropriate for supervisors or workers with safety responsibility. A Construction 10-hour card alone is not the Construction 30-hour card a contract may require. Nor does a General Industry 30-hour card automatically satisfy a Construction 30-hour requirement. Check the exact wording with the party setting the requirement.

OSHA’s FAQ describes a narrow route where the same trainer can add 20 hours to a 10-hour course within six months, with the original 10-hour card returned. Do not assume any 10-hour course can be topped up later with a different provider. Confirm that arrangement with the trainer and the party requiring the card before relying on it.

After training: check the floor and the records

Outreach training is an orientation, not a check of your jobsite. Under 29 CFR 1926.21(b)(2), the employer must instruct each employee on recognizing and avoiding unsafe conditions and on the rules that apply to the employee’s work. Put the work plan beside the written procedure. Identify who sets the control, who checks it and what record shows the crew was instructed.

FAQ

Can a construction supervisor take OSHA 30-hour Outreach online?

Yes. Check that the provider appears under Construction 30-hour on OSHA’s current authorized online-provider list, and confirm the specific course before paying.

Is an online OSHA 30-hour Construction card accepted on every jobsite?

Do not assume it is. Ask the employer, contract administrator or other organization requiring the card what course and proof it accepts before enrolling.

Does OSHA require a 30-hour card for every construction supervisor?

No. Federal OSHA says Outreach training is voluntary, although employers, contracts and some jurisdictions require it. Job-specific training obligations still apply.

Can an OSHA 10-hour Construction card replace a 30-hour card?

No, not by itself. OSHA describes a limited same-trainer process for adding 20 hours within six months, but check the card requirement before relying on it.

When should the OSHA Outreach student card arrive?

OSHA requires the trainer to issue it directly to the student within 90 days of class completion. Ask the jobsite whether it accepts a temporary completion certificate in the meantime.

Does an OSHA 30-hour Construction student card expire?

OSHA says the Construction student course-completion card has no expiration date. A trainer’s authorization card does have an expiration date, and an employer or jobsite can require additional training.

One last thing

Choose the course your supervisor can complete and your project will accept. Then check whether that supervisor can point to the actual control on the jobsite. The card records a course; it does not verify the work.
