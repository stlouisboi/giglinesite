The direct answer

An OSHA safety consultant should price the work after the scope is clear. A Safety Walkthrough, a Documentation Readiness Review, and Corrective Action Implementation solve different problems. If those three are bundled into one vague line item, you do not know what you are buying.

WHAT I NEED TO KNOW FIRST

  • Do you need the floor checked, the paperwork checked, or known gaps fixed?
  • How many buildings, shifts, and work areas are in scope?
  • Are your records current, scattered, or missing?
  • Do you need a one-time project or ongoing maintenance?

Why this matters on your floor

I see the same problem when plant managers compare quotes. One proposal covers a walk through the building. Another includes a document review and a written findings list. A third includes hands-on correction work. The prices look far apart because the scopes are far apart.

Start with the problem you need solved. Then compare the work line by line.

Safety Walkthrough

This is the FIND work. I look at the floor, the equipment, and the way the job is actually being done. Facility size matters, but complexity matters more. One production area with several energy sources can take more work than a larger, simpler warehouse.

Documentation Readiness Review

This is where I compare the paperwork to the operation. I look for the records, programs, logs, and training proof that should support the work. A clean file set takes less time to review than two years of scattered records.

Corrective Action Implementation

This is BUILD work. The findings already exist. The job is to turn them into fixes, assigned actions, and proof that can be maintained. The quote depends on the number of findings and how much hands-on work each fix requires.

How I define the work

I use two simple sequences. They keep the scope from getting vague.

FINDWalk the floor. Review the records. Name the gaps.
BUILDTurn findings into fixes, controls, and usable proof.
MAINTAINKeep the controls current through Quarterly Maintenance or an Annual Control Partner engagement.

The same work can be read another way: Floor, Findings, Fixes, Proof. If a proposal skips one of those steps, ask why.

What changes the quote

  • Number of locations. Every site has its own floor, records, and people.
  • Facility complexity. Powered equipment, chemical use, fleet activity, and multiple shifts add review time.
  • Condition of the records. Current files are faster to review than missing or scattered files.
  • Number of known findings. Corrective Action Implementation is scoped around the fixes that still need to be completed.
  • One-time work or ongoing control. Quarterly Maintenance and Annual Control Partner work are different from a single visit.
  • Travel and scheduling. Distance and access windows affect on-site work.
A low quote is not useful if it leaves the floor, the findings, or the proof out of scope.

When outside help makes sense

Bring in outside help when your team needs an independent look, when the paperwork no longer matches the floor, or when known findings are sitting open. You should leave the engagement knowing what was checked, what was found, what still needs to be fixed, and what proof belongs in the file.

If you cannot get that answer from the scope, the quote is not ready.

How to compare two quotes

Put the scopes side by side. Check the locations, work areas, records, findings, deliverables, and follow-up. Do not compare two totals until those items match.

I would also ask one direct question: does this quote stop at findings, or does it include help building the fix? That answer changes the work.

Questions plant managers ask me

Can you quote the work without visiting the site?

I can discuss the likely scope first. A firm quote still needs enough information about the facility, records, work areas, and outcome you need.

What is the difference between a Safety Walkthrough and a Documentation Readiness Review?

A Safety Walkthrough checks the floor. A Documentation Readiness Review checks the records and proof that support the floor.

What if we already have a findings list?

Then Corrective Action Implementation may be the right scope. I would review the open items, define the fixes, and identify the proof needed to close them.

What if we need ongoing help after the first project?

Quarterly Maintenance and Annual Control Partner are the ongoing options. The right one depends on how often the controls, records, and open actions need attention.

Is a Compliance Readiness Visit the same as a Safety Walkthrough?

No. A Compliance Readiness Visit connects the floor, findings, fixes, and proof. A Safety Walkthrough is focused on what is happening on the floor.
