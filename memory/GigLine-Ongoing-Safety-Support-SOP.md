## 1. Purpose

This SOP defines how GigLine Safety and Compliance delivers the Ongoing Safety Support service. It ensures every client receives the same quality of monthly follow-through regardless of when they onboard, and creates a defensible record of what was and was not delivered under the agreed scope.

This document is internal. It is not distributed to clients. Clients receive the web page copy, the client agreement, the monthly management summary, and the corrective-action tracker.

---

## 2. Service definition

Ongoing Safety Support is a monthly, retainer-style engagement for small employers (generally 10 to 50 employees at one primary location) that need consistent safety follow-through but are not ready to hire a full-time safety manager.

### 2.1 What GigLine provides each month

At the starting scope, GigLine provides the following per calendar month:

1. One scheduled on-site visit, up to 2.5 hours on-site.
2. One facility safety walkthrough during the on-site visit.
3. Corrective-action tracker review and updates.
4. Review of selected training, inspection, and program records.
5. One toolbox talk or short safety-meeting resource.
6. One monthly management review meeting (phone, video, or in-person, at Vince's discretion based on distance and urgency).
7. One concise leadership summary document delivered to the client contact.
8. Up to 45 minutes per month of non-emergency remote support, by email or scheduled phone/video consultation. Limited incident next-step guidance, when provided, uses this same allowance and excludes investigation, on-site response, regulator contact, and after-hours availability.

Total capacity: up to eight hours of GigLine time per month.

### 2.2 What GigLine does not provide under the starting scope

The following are separate services or require an appropriate specialist. They are billed and scheduled separately from the monthly retainer.

- Major incident investigations (fatalities, hospitalizations, amputations, or any OSHA reportable event)
- OSHA inspection representation
- Environmental compliance
- Engineering analyses, controls design, ventilation calculations
- Industrial hygiene sampling
- Equipment corrections, purchases, or installations
- Complete written program development from a blank slate
- Historical record reconstruction
- Contractor prequalification portal management (ISNetworld, Avetta, Veriforce, etc.)
- Specialized training delivery (forklift operator certification classes, respirator fit testing, first-aid/CPR)
- Legal representation or interpretation
- After-hours or weekend on-site response

### 2.3 Pricing at time of publication

- Starting monthly fee: $1,650/month
- Compliance Readiness Visit (CRV), required initial: $2,500
- Standard 90-day initial commitment: $4,950 (three monthly payments)
- Combined initial investment for a new client: $7,450 (CRV plus three months)

Prices are refreshed on the web page. This SOP references the pricing model, not the absolute numbers, so it does not require a rewrite when prices adjust.

## 3. Prerequisites, no engagement begins without these

GigLine does not sign an Ongoing Safety Support agreement until all of the following are true.

### 3.1 The client passed a fit call

The client completed the fit-call form on `/ongoing-safety-support`, and Vince reviewed it within one business day. The fit call determined that the operation is a reasonable fit for the service (10 to 50 employees, one primary location, willing leadership, within 45 minutes of Kernersville at the starting scope).

If any red flags surfaced, they are documented in the fit-call notes:

- Owner or plant manager is looking for a scapegoat rather than a partner
- Recent fatality, hospitalization, or willful citation
- Multiple locations or shifts at the starting scope
- History of ignoring safety consultants or previous corrective actions
- Distance or hazard level requiring custom scope

Red flags do not automatically disqualify a client, but they require Vince to price and scope differently or to decline the engagement.

### 3.2 The client accepted a Compliance Readiness Visit

Every new recurring client begins with a paid CRV or an approved onboarding assessment. The CRV establishes the baseline condition of the floor, the documents, and the open risks.

Exception: GigLine may accept a recent equivalent assessment only when Vince determines that it provides a reliable baseline. This decision is documented in writing before the ongoing agreement is signed.

### 3.3 The written agreement is signed and the first month is paid

The client agreement includes:

- Scope of the monthly service (mirrors Section 2.1 of this SOP)
- Out-of-scope items (mirrors Section 2.2)
- Fees, invoicing schedule, and payment terms
- Initial term (typically 90 days)
- Termination provisions
- Employer responsibility statement
- Confidentiality provisions
- Limitation of liability
- No-guarantee language matching the web page

The 90-day initial term begins on the first day of the first paid service month, not the CRV date.

## 4. The monthly service cycle

Every service month follows the same rhythm. It is designed so that if Vince is unavailable for any reason, another qualified professional could pick up the client file and deliver the next cycle without interviewing the client.

### 4.1 Week 1, prepare

Before the on-site visit, Vince completes:

1. Review the prior month's leadership summary.
2. Pull the corrective-action tracker and list every item due, in progress, or overdue.
3. Confirm the on-site visit date, arrival time, and expected client contact via email.
4. Choose the walkthrough focus for this month (see Section 4.5).
5. Choose the record-review focus for this month (see Section 4.6).
6. Prepare or select the toolbox talk resource.

### 4.2 Week 2 or 3, on-site visit

The on-site visit is capped at 2.5 hours. Vince arrives with:

- Rugged tablet loaded with the corrective-action tracker
- Digital camera (or tablet camera) for photo documentation
- The current facility hazard map, if one exists
- Printed toolbox talk resource
- Client-specific reference documents (SDS binder location, previous walkthrough notes, applicable OSHA standards for this operation)

On-site time is spent as follows (approximate):

- 20 minutes, arrival, greet client contact, review agenda, walk to first area
- 60 to 75 minutes, facility safety walkthrough
- 20 to 30 minutes, corrective-action review with client contact
- 10 to 15 minutes, capture photos and notes for open items
- 5 to 10 minutes, close-out with client contact

If a scheduled visit exceeds 2.5 hours because of an active issue, Vince notes the overage and either bills separately or applies the additional time to next month's capacity (see Section 4.9).

### 4.3 Week 3, off-site work

Within three business days of the on-site visit, Vince completes:

1. Update the corrective-action tracker with the day's findings.
2. Take any records that were pulled during the visit off-site or scan them, then return originals.
3. Draft the monthly management summary.
4. Log the on-site hours and any remote support hours consumed.

### 4.4 Week 4, delivery and management review

The monthly management review is scheduled during Week 4. The delivery packet is:

1. Updated corrective-action tracker (PDF or shared document link)
2. Monthly management summary (see Section 5.2 template)
3. Toolbox talk resource for the month
4. Any records reviewed, with brief notes on gaps and recommendations

The management review meeting is 30 minutes maximum. Agenda:

- What changed since last month
- What closed
- What remained open and why
- What GigLine recommends for next month
- Any decisions needed from client leadership

### 4.5 Walkthrough focus rotation

To keep monthly walkthroughs from becoming repetitive scans, Vince rotates the focus. Suggested rotation for a general-industry manufacturing client:

- Month 1, hazard communication and chemical storage
- Month 2, machine guarding and general workshop equipment
- Month 3, powered industrial trucks (forklifts) and pedestrian interaction
- Month 4, electrical safety and extension cord practices
- Month 5, walking-working surfaces, housekeeping, egress
- Month 6, personal protective equipment inventory and condition
- Month 7, lockout tagout program observation
- Month 8, emergency preparedness (alarms, evacuation, extinguishers)
- Month 9, respiratory or hearing conservation, if applicable
- Month 10, contractor management and visitor safety
- Month 11, recordkeeping (OSHA 300, 301, 300A posting), training records
- Month 12, annual program review and next-year planning

Every walkthrough also captures anything visibly urgent regardless of the month's focus.

### 4.6 Record review focus rotation

Records are reviewed on a rotating schedule so the entire program gets examined every 12 months without overwhelming any single visit. Rotation aligns with the walkthrough focus above.

For each reviewed record set, Vince notes:

- What exists (dates, coverage, signatures)
- What is missing
- What is out of date
- What GigLine recommends

### 4.7 Corrective-action tracker standard

Every finding gets one row with the following fields:

| Field | Description |
|---|---|
| Finding ID | Sequential, unique per client (GL-CLIENT-YYYY-###) |
| Discovered date | ISO date, YYYY-MM-DD |
| Source | Walkthrough, record review, incident, near miss, client-reported |
| Standard | Applicable OSHA CFR or internal program reference |
| Hazard description | Plain-language, one to two sentences |
| Photo reference | Filename or link, no PII in filename |
| Severity | Low, Medium, High, Critical |
| Priority | Immediate, 30-day, 60-day, 90-day, program-level |
| Owner | Client-side person accountable |
| Target date | ISO date |
| Current status | Open, In progress, Verified, Closed, Deferred |
| Verification method | Photo, document, retraining record, direct observation |
| Verified date | ISO date when GigLine confirmed correction |
| Notes | Any context, decisions, or scope questions |

The tracker is the single source of truth. Nothing gets "handled" without a row.

### 4.8 Capacity management

Every service month has an eight-hour budget. Vince tracks time in the following buckets:

- On-site (walkthrough + client review + travel-to-site cannot exceed 2.5 hours combined)
- Documentation prep (drafting summary, updating tracker)
- Remote support (email replies, scheduled calls)
- Management review meeting

If a month appears likely to exceed capacity, Vince notifies the client in writing before overrunning. Options offered:

1. Move an agreed priority to the next service month.
2. Reduce another included activity this month.
3. Issue a separately quoted change order for the additional work.

Additional work does not begin without written client authorization.

### 4.9 Time-tracking discipline

Every hour or partial hour is logged in the client's monthly capacity ledger:

- Date
- Start and end time
- Activity type (on-site, prep, remote, meeting, admin)
- Corrective-action IDs touched (if applicable)
- Notes

The ledger accompanies each month's management summary. It supports transparent capacity conversations and defends the scope of the engagement if ever challenged.

## 5. Documentation standards

### 5.1 File naming

All client artifacts use the following convention:

```
GL-[CLIENT-CODE]-[YYYY-MM]-[artifact-type]-[optional-suffix].pdf
```

Examples:
- `GL-ACME-2026-09-management-summary.pdf`
- `GL-ACME-2026-09-tracker.pdf`
- `GL-ACME-2026-09-toolbox-talk-lockout.pdf`

Client codes are 3 to 6 characters, uppercase, no spaces, assigned at the CRV.

### 5.2 Monthly management summary template

Every management summary contains the same seven sections in this order:

1. **Period covered.** Calendar month, on-site visit date, delivery date.
2. **What closed.** Bulleted list of every finding verified as corrected this month, with the tracker ID.
3. **What is on track.** Findings in progress with target dates in the near future.
4. **What is overdue and why.** Findings past their target date, with the current owner and the reason for the delay.
5. **What GigLine observed this month.** Plain-language walkthrough narrative, 2 to 5 short paragraphs.
6. **What GigLine recommends next month.** The proposed focus for the next visit and any decisions needed from leadership.
7. **Capacity summary.** Hours used this month, remaining allowance if any, any change-order flags.

The summary is under three pages. Executives skim. Vince writes for scanning.

### 5.3 Photo standards

Every photo taken on a client site:

- Is stored in the client's private folder, not on shared or public storage
- Has no personally-identifying information in the filename
- Is timestamped by camera metadata (not manually)
- Shows enough context to identify the location without leaving room for confusion

Photos of employees are avoided. When unavoidable (a hazardous work practice in progress), faces are cropped or blurred before the photo lands in the tracker.

### 5.4 Retention

Client files are retained for a minimum of six years after the last service month, matching common OSHA training-record retention. Vince notifies the client 60 days before purge and offers to hand off the file to the client contact.

## 6. Boundaries, escalation, and incidents

### 6.1 Non-emergency remote support allowance

The 45-minute monthly remote allowance covers:

- Answering client email or text questions on program applicability
- Reviewing a short draft (policy paragraph, toolbox talk, PPE selection matrix)
- Guidance on how to document a specific record
- Short next-step guidance after a minor incident or near-miss

It does not cover:

- On-site incident response
- Investigation of the root cause of an event
- Regulator contact of any kind
- Final recordability or reportability determinations under 29 CFR 1904
- OSHA inspection assistance
- After-hours or weekend availability
- Emergency triage

### 6.2 Incident escalation ladder

When a client reports a workplace incident, Vince responds within one business day and asks the following four questions in order:

1. Is the injured person receiving appropriate medical care right now?
2. Was there a fatality, hospitalization, amputation, or loss of an eye?
3. Has OSHA or another regulator contacted the client?
4. Is the scene preserved or has it been altered?

If the answers to #2 or #3 are yes, Vince informs the client in writing that this event exceeds the standard remote-support allowance and requires a separately scoped incident-support engagement. Vince offers to help scope that engagement or to refer the client to a qualified specialist.

If the answer to #2 and #3 are no, Vince may provide up to the 45-minute allowance in non-emergency next-step guidance:

- What to document while memory is fresh
- Whether the event is likely OSHA recordable (advisory only, final determination is the employer's)
- What to consider for immediate corrective action
- When to expect a follow-up conversation in the next scheduled service window

Vince never contacts OSHA on the client's behalf under the standard monthly service.

### 6.3 OSHA inspection request

If the client reports an active or imminent OSHA inspection, Vince informs the client in writing that on-site inspection representation is out of scope, offers to help the client find qualified representation (legal counsel or an EHS specialist with inspection-representation experience), and offers to provide any records GigLine has on file to the client for their use during the inspection.

### 6.4 Scope creep management

Every month Vince asks: "Did I do work this month that was not in the agreement, and did the client know I was doing extra?"

If yes, Vince documents the overage in the capacity summary and either:

- Absorbs the overage as a one-time courtesy and notes it in the file, or
- Issues a written change-order proposal for the additional work.

Absorbing overages more than once per quarter for the same client is a signal that the scope needs to be renegotiated at the next renewal.

## 7. Renewal, scope adjustments, and termination

### 7.1 90-day initial term review

At day 75 of the initial term, Vince and the client hold a 45-minute review meeting. Agenda:

- Original baseline versus current condition
- What worked in the monthly rhythm
- What did not fit and needs adjustment
- Recommendation for continued engagement, scope adjustment, or wind-down

If the client continues, a month-to-month agreement takes over on day 91. If either party ends the engagement, the wind-down process in Section 7.4 applies.

### 7.2 Annual review

Every 12 months of continuous engagement, Vince and the client hold a 60-minute annual review. Deliverable is a written year-in-review document covering:

- Findings opened, closed, verified, and outstanding
- Program areas that moved from unmanaged to managed
- Incidents, near-misses, and recordable events during the year
- Training completion rates for the year
- Recommended focus for the next 12 months
- Recommended scope adjustments if any

The annual review is included in the standard monthly capacity. It does not trigger an additional invoice.

### 7.3 Scope-adjustment triggers

The monthly scope is renegotiated if any of the following occurs:

- A second location is added, requested, or discovered
- A second shift is added, requested, or discovered
- Employee count exceeds 50 for two consecutive months
- A high-hazard process is added (new welding operation, new solvent process, new confined space, hot-work permit program)
- Travel distance exceeds 45 minutes from Kernersville under normal driving conditions
- A recordable OSHA event or citation

The revised scope is proposed in writing before the next service month.

### 7.4 Termination

Either party may terminate the month-to-month agreement with 30 days written notice. Upon termination, GigLine:

1. Completes any in-progress verification of open corrective actions if the target date falls inside the 30-day notice period.
2. Delivers a final management summary covering the notice period.
3. Provides a written engagement transition document listing every open corrective action, its current status, the responsible client-side owner, and the recommended next steps.
4. Returns or securely destroys any client records held off-site, per the client's written preference.

Termination for cause (client refuses to complete corrective actions of a life-safety nature, or the client asks GigLine to falsify a record) is immediate and documented in writing.

## 8. Quality control

### 8.1 Internal audit

Once per quarter, Vince audits three random client files against this SOP. The audit checklist:

- Is there a signed agreement on file?
- Is the CRV on file?
- Is the tracker current within the last service month?
- Do the last three management summaries exist and follow the template?
- Are photo files named per Section 5.1?
- Is the capacity ledger current?
- Are any overdue items missing an escalation note?
- Are any recommendations from the last annual review being followed?

Any gap found in an audit is closed within 10 business days.

### 8.2 Client satisfaction check

At months 3, 6, 9, and 12 of engagement, Vince asks the client contact one question:

*"On a scale of 1 to 5, is the monthly rhythm giving you what you needed?"*

Any answer of 3 or lower triggers a 20-minute follow-up call within one week to understand and adjust.

### 8.3 Continuous improvement

At every annual review, Vince notes one thing that would have made the year easier for the client and one thing that would have made it easier for GigLine. Recurring themes become inputs to the next version of this SOP or to the standard client agreement.

- **GigLine provides safety assessment, documentation review, corrective-action tracking, coordination, and management reporting within the agreed scope.**
- **The employer retains authority and responsibility for its workplace, employees, equipment, supervision, corrective actions, and compliance obligations.**
